## Hybrid Rocket Trailer — BYU Rocketry Club / Capstone

**What:** A functional hybrid rocket trailer that transports nitrous oxide (N₂O) and can launch a hybrid rocket from its back, built to conform to all safety standards and rules for the Spaceport launch competition.

**How:**
- Created 3D models for the electrical system in SolidWorks
- Created schematics and high-level system models in KiCad and Visio
- Built and maintained the bill of materials
- Designed control systems and safety interlocks for the trailer systems

**Results:** Electronics subsystem team leader on a team of 10 — led meetings and drove project progression. Trailer completed and functioning on the February 2025 timeline. (Capstone role: Mechatronics Team Lead, Aug 2024 – May 2025, leading a 3-person multidisciplinary sub-team; system delivered on schedule and budget. Electrical testing included waterproofing work for the trailer.)

## Automotive Mechatronics Demo — ROHM Semiconductor

**What:** A working LED driver demo that controls automotive lights and motors from one microcontroller (Technical Marketing Engineer Intern, Novi, MI, May – Aug 2024).

**How:**
- Modeled and analyzed the PCB design and circuits in KiCad
- Wrote C code in the Arduino IDE to control the LEDs and motor from the single MCU

**Results:** The demo functioned as designed and reduced electrical complexity by 75%. It was presented at the Electronica conference in Germany in November. The PCB designs from this internship shipped at scale.

## Ping Pong Shooting Robot — BYU

**What:** An autonomous robot able to acquire ping pong balls, store them, read IR sensors, and shoot different-colored balls into corresponding nets.

**How:**
- Helped create the 3D models in SolidWorks
- Wrote C code for communication between the robot and its environment (sensors)
- Tested components: battery, stepper motors, and the shooter mechanism

**Results:** The robot shot with 85% accuracy and functioned according to design — 14 balls into the nets in the allotted 1 minute 30 seconds.

## Pipsqueak Engine — BYU

**What:** A single-piston engine attached to a flywheel, powered by compressed air, designed to run continuously under increasing air pressure until failure.

**How:**
- Helped create 3D models and drawings in SolidWorks
- Manufactured parts using lathe, mill, and threading operations
- Helped create the bill of materials and documented the manufacturing processes

**Results:** Reached a high of 30 psi — higher than expected — and functioned as intended, delivering rotational energy from compressed air through run-to-failure testing.

**How:** Aerodynamic analysis and design iteration in XFLR; documented in a full design report with a formal design review presentation (both on file).

**Results:** Completed design report and design review for the course.
